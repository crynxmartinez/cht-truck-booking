02 — Booking rules

How the calendar actually blocks

One booking blocks one truck for three consecutive calendar days starting on the pickup date. The third day is the return-and-rest day: the renter brings it back, you inspect and refuel, and the truck goes out again the next morning.

Worked example — Truck A booked for Monday

MonPickup
Day 1
TueIn use
Day 2
WedReturn
+ rest
ThuFree
again
FriFree

Truck B stays fully bookable across all five days. A date shows as available while at least one truck has no overlapping block — so most dates offer capacity 2, and only go dark when both trucks are out.

Confirm this reading

You said “3 days period — the booking date and allowance so the truck can rest.” I have built that as pickup + 2 days, return due on day 3, free on day 4. If you actually mean three full use days with a separate rest day on top, it is a one-line change: RENTAL_BLOCK_DAYS = 4. Say the word and I flip it.

  • Lead time. Earliest bookable date is tomorrow. Same-day is off by default so nobody books at 11 PM for 6 AM.
  • Horizon. Calendar scrolls three months forward, no further.
  • Blackouts. You can take either truck off the calendar for a service window from the CRM, and it stops being offered immediately.
  • Race protection. Two people submitting for the last truck in the same second is handled inside a database transaction that locks the truck row — one wins, the other gets a clean “that date just went” message rather than a double booking.

05 — Contracts

The PDFs become web pages

I read all ten sample PDFs. They are three documents wearing different filenames:

DocumentPagesWhat it contains
Truck Rental Agreement5Data page (renter, addresses, licence, DOB, insurance, truck no. + plate + year, dispatched/received condition boxes), the 13-clause Rental Agreement terms, and the 13-section Waiver & Release of Liability
Additional Driver Agreement5Same terms and waiver, but the data page collects the second driver plus “name of the person who booked the rental”
Truck A vs Truck B—Identical except the truck block. A is plate 764302D / 2022; B is plate 705921E / 2021 and carries a Date & Time Out / In pair instead of Return / Pickup dates.

So we build one contract template with the truck block swapped in from the database, not two. Renter opens /sign/<token> and gets:

  1. Their details, already filled. Name, phone, email carried over from the booking. They complete only what we cannot know: employer, old and new address, licence number, date of birth, insurance carrier, contact and policy number.
  2. Initials on the damage waiver. The $2,500 comprehensive/collision notice gets its own initials box, exactly as the paper form does.
  3. The full terms, scrollable. Both the 13 rental clauses and the waiver, verbatim. Signing is disabled until they have scrolled to the bottom — that is what makes “I have read and agree” defensible.
  4. Additional driver — yes or no. If yes, we ask for that driver’s name, mobile and email right there, then send them their own link. The renter never has to forward anything.
  5. Signature. Draw with a finger or type a name, plus the date. Diana’s counter-signature is pre-applied from the stored graphic.
  6. Locked PDF. On submit we generate the finished document server-side, store it, attach it to the confirmation email, and show it on the CRM card.
Why this holds up better than DocuSign for you

We record the signer’s IP, browser, exact timestamp and a hash of the precise contract wording they saw. Version the terms and you can prove, two years later, what a specific person agreed to — with none of the per-envelope cost.

08 — Timing

One clock, set to Murrieta

Vercel runs cron in UTC, and California moves twice a year. Chasing that with a fixed UTC hour means your 6 AM text arrives at 5 AM for half the year. So: a single job runs every hour, asks what time it is in America/Los_Angeles, and dispatches whatever is due.

Local hourJobGuard
06:00Pickup morning messagePickup date is today, stage is Confirmed, not already sent
07:00Return-day messageReturn date is today, stage is In Use
14:00“Did you get the truck?”Pickup was today, no pickup checklist yet
09:00Contract nudges, overdue sweep, retention purgeOnce daily
This needs Vercel Pro

The Hobby plan allows two cron jobs and only daily granularity — an hourly tick is not possible on it. Either budget $20/month for Vercel Pro, or point a free external scheduler (cron-job.org, or a GHL recurring workflow) at a secured /api/cron/tick endpoint. Both work; Pro is tidier. Your call, and it is the only recurring cost in the build.

09 — Storage

WebP in, oldest out

Exactly as you asked, with guardrails so the cleanup can never delete something you still need.

  • Converted twice. The browser downscales to 1600px and encodes WebP at quality 82 before upload — a 4 MB phone photo lands around 180 KB. The server re-encodes anything that slipped through, so a licence photo can never arrive as a 12 MB HEIC.
  • Private by default. Blobs are not publicly listable; the CRM serves them through short-lived signed URLs. Driver’s licences should not be sitting on a guessable URL.
  • The 80% rule. A nightly job totals blob usage against your configured quota. Over 80%, it deletes the oldest eligible photos until back under 70% — deleting to exactly 80% means it runs again tomorrow.

What “eligible” means

AssetKept untilReason
Signed contract PDFsNever purgedYour legal record. A few hundred KB each — they will never be the problem.
Licence & insuranceCompleted + 30 daysSensitive ID documents. Holding them longer is a liability, not an asset.
Checklist photosCompleted + 90 daysLong enough for a damage claim to surface.
Anything on an open bookingUntouchableNot Completed, flagged Overdue, or marked Needs review — the purge skips it entirely.

Every deletion is logged, and the CRM shows a storage gauge so “we are at 80%” is never a surprise.

Two things to check on the GHL side

The private-integration token needs scopes for contacts.write, conversations.write, conversations/message.write and locations/customFields.write. And the location must already have a phone number and email service connected — the API sends through your existing setup, it does not provide one.

If sending direct proves fiddly, the fallback is to fire an inbound webhook into a GHL workflow and let the workflow send using your own templates. Same result, more clicking. I would try direct first.
